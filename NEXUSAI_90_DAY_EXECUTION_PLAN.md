# NexusAI — 90-Day Execution Plan

This plan turns the vision into actions for one founder plus, at most, one or two engineers. It front-loads **evidence** (weeks 1–4) and treats code as something to earn the right to write. Targets are **[Recommendation]** values to calibrate; none is a forecast.

Principle: **commitments over compliments.** A signed letter of intent, a paid pilot or a card on file counts. "That sounds great" counts for nothing.

---

## 1. Positioning and the message to test

**Positioning statement (to test, not to assume):**
> For founders and owner-managers of small services businesses in Egypt and the Gulf, NexusAI is the AI chief of staff that remembers everything you promised and everything you are waiting on, tells you each morning on WhatsApp what needs you, and chases what is slipping — in Arabic or English, with your approval on every message, for a fixed monthly price.

**Three headline variants for the landing page experiment**
1. "Never drop the ball with a client again." (outcome)
2. "Your morning brief on WhatsApp: what you owe, what you're owed." (channel and habit)
3. "A secretary that follows up so you don't have to." (job)

**Measuring ROI (what the pitch and the pilot report must show)**
- Commitments captured per week and share closed on time (before vs during the pilot, taken from the user's own sent mail and calendar, with their consent).
- Follow-ups sent via the assistant and replies received.
- Minutes per day the owner spends on triage (self-reported weekly plus time-to-first-action after the brief).
- Overdue items older than 7 days: count at the start and after 4 weeks.
- Price comparison: hours of human assistant time displaced multiplied by a local hourly rate (the user supplies the rate; do not assume it).

## 2. Validation strategy

### 2.1 Customer interview guide (30 minutes, 25+ interviews in weeks 1–3)
Targets: owners, GMs, partners of 5–50-person services businesses in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE; mix of agencies, consultancies, brokerages and clinic managers; at least 8 who currently pay a human assistant.

Open with the past, not the future:
1. "Walk me through yesterday morning: what did you open first, and what did you do in the first hour?"
2. "Tell me about the last time a client or colleague said you hadn't followed up. What happened? What did it cost?"
3. "Where do your promises and to-dos live today — inbox, WhatsApp, notebook, someone's head, a tool?"
4. "How do you decide what to answer first? What slips through?"
5. "Do you have an assistant, human or software? What do you pay? What does it do badly?"
6. "Which of Fyxer, Motion, Copilot, Gemini, Superhuman, or Lindy have you tried? Why did you stop (or continue)?"
7. "How much of this work happens on WhatsApp? Voice notes? In which language?"
8. "Whose email is it okay for software to read? What would make you refuse?"
9. "If something sent an email in your name wrongly, what would you do? What would you need to feel safe?"
Close with commitment tests (only after the above):
10. "If this existed and worked as described, how would you pay for it today? What is the price at which you would hesitate? At which you would say it's too cheap to trust?" (Van Westendorp style: record the four price points.)
11. "Would you try it for four weeks with your real inbox, and pay $X at the end if it saved you time? Can we book the setup call now?"
12. "Who else should I speak to?"

Rules: never describe the product before question 9; record verbatim quotes; log the *last real incident* for each interviewee; score each interview 0–3 on pain, current spend and commitment.

### 2.2 Competitor comparison (done once, then refreshed monthly)
A one-page table of Fyxer, Motion, Reclaim, Lindy, Superhuman, Shortwave, Copilot business: price, channels, languages, what happens when it is wrong, cancellation experience (create a free account on three and note the exit flow), and the single sentence each uses to sell. Verify prices from the vendor pages again before using them in any customer-facing material (see the market research for what is verified and what is secondary).

### 2.3 Landing page experiments (weeks 2–5)
- One page, two languages (Arabic and English), pricing visible, waitlist with a mandatory field "what slips through today?".
- Drive 300–500 qualified visits from direct outreach, founder groups and LinkedIn (not broad ads).
- **Experiments:** headline A/B/C above; price display $19 / $29 / $49 for Professional (random per visitor); CTA "Join the pilot" vs "Get early access".
- **Success signals:** ≥ 5% of qualified visitors join the waitlist; ≥ 30% of those answer the open question; at least 10 respond to a follow-up call request.

### 2.4 Demo scenarios (use real data with consent, or a staged inbox)
1. **The dropped promise:** inbox with an email where the owner promised a proposal by Thursday → the ledger shows it with the source and the due date → the morning brief lists it.
2. **The silent client:** a quote sent six days ago without reply → the assistant drafts a polite nudge in the owner's tone (Arabic and English versions) → one-tap approval.
3. **Meeting prep:** next meeting with a client → prep card: last three interactions, open commitments both ways.
4. **Voice in, order out:** a WhatsApp voice note in Egyptian Arabic ("ذكّرني أبعت العرض لمحمد بكرة") → ledger entry created → confirmation reply.
5. **Safety:** an email trying to make the assistant send data elsewhere is shown as untrusted and nothing is sent without approval.

### 2.5 Pilot design (weeks 4–12)
- **Participants:** 10 businesses, 1–3 users each; selection from the interview pool by commitment score.
- **Terms:** four-week paid pilot at $29 per user (Egypt $17), refundable if the user says it did not help; they connect their own Gmail/Calendar; data deletion on request.
- **Concierge first:** until the ledger is automated, the founder reviews extracted items daily and fixes them (this also produces the labelled evaluation set). Be explicit with participants that early quality is supervised.
- **Weekly check-in (20 minutes):** show the ledger, ask what was wrong, what was missing, what they ignored.
- **Instrumentation:** log (with consent) captures, corrections, approvals, brief opens, WhatsApp replies; no reading of email content beyond what the product needs.

### 2.6 Feedback collection
In-product thumbs on each ledger item and brief ("right / wrong / not useful"); a weekly one-question survey ("How disappointed would you be if this stopped tomorrow?"); a monthly call with each pilot account; a shared tracker where every complaint is tagged (accuracy, trust, language, channel, price, other).

### 2.7 Product-market-fit indicators (calibrated for this stage, not for a mature product)
- ≥ 40% of active users answer "very disappointed" to the survey question (a commonly used benchmark; use it as a signal, not proof).
- ≥ 50% of pilot businesses pay after four weeks at the stated price.
- Users open the brief on at least 4 days per week.
- Corrections per 100 ledger items fall week over week.
- Unprompted referrals: at least 3 pilot users introduce another business.
- Cost per active user under the plan in the business model.

## 3. Customer acquisition: the first 10 paying customers

**Best initial segment [Hypothesis]:** owner-managers of small services businesses (agencies, consultancies, brokerages) in Egypt and the Gulf who use Google Workspace and run on WhatsApp. They have the pain, the authority to buy alone, a short sales cycle and no assistant on payroll.

| Channel | How | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct outreach (founder-led)** | Personal LinkedIn messages and WhatsApp intros to 150 owner-managers (warm network first), offering a 20-minute conversation about "dropped follow-ups", not a pitch | 25 interviews, 10 pilots | Highest conversion; do it yourself |
| **Founder and startup communities** | Cairo, Riyadh, Dubai founder groups, accelerator alumni networks; offer a free "follow-up audit" of last week's sent mail | 10–15 warm intros | Give value before asking |
| **Cold email** | Short, specific, in Arabic or English, one question ("Who chases your clients' replies?") | 300 sends, expect low reply rates | Useful mainly to test message wording |
| **Product demonstration** | 15-minute screen-share on the prospect's own (consented) mailbox or a staged one | 10 demos | Shows the ledger in minutes |
| **Paid pilot programme** | The structure in §2.5; first 10 customers are pilots that convert | 10 | A paid pilot is the strongest commitment signal |
| **Partnerships** | Accountants, business consultants, coworking spaces, Google Workspace resellers: a referral fee in exchange for introductions | 3 partners | Slow but compounding |
| **Free trial** | Only for self-serve Personal tier after Phase 1; not for pilots | — | Avoid "free forever" users who never pay |

**Sequence:** weeks 1–3 interviews and outreach → weeks 3–5 waitlist and pilot invitations → weeks 5–12 pilot cohort and conversion. **The first 10 paying customers are the pilot businesses that pay at the end of their four weeks.**

### Getting to the first 100 customers (months 3–10)
1. **Referral loop from pilots:** every pilot user gets a short "introduce one peer" ask at week 3; offer one month free to both.
2. **Content that proves the pain:** a monthly "Follow-up audit report" (anonymised statistics from pilots with consent): how many promises were open, how many closed on time; posted on LinkedIn and in founder groups.
3. **Partner channel:** accountants and consultants who see clients' operations and can recommend a tool; 20% recurring referral share for 12 months [Assumption].
4. **Community presence:** talks and live demos in founder meetups; a monthly webinar "Chief of staff in 15 minutes".
5. **Local SEO and Arabic content** around "متابعة العملاء", "سكرتير ذكي" (check search terms with a keyword tool before investing).
6. **Self-serve Personal tier** (Phase 2) feeds Professional upgrades; cap spend on paid acquisition until payback is proven.
Rule: do not scale spend until month-3 retention ≥ 80% and gross margin ≥ 70% (Gate G3).

## 4. The 90-day calendar

### Weeks 1–2: Learn and unblock
- **Founder:** book and run the first 10 interviews; build the 150-name outreach list; draft the landing page copy (Arabic and English).
- **Platform (start now, long lead):**
  - Decide the legal entity and the payment route (confirm Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy or a regional processor can actually pay out to you; see the business model).
  - Create the Google Cloud project settings for a verified app: privacy policy, terms, homepage, a demo video script; decide the **minimum scopes** for the MVP and list which are restricted (see the audit). Check the current security-assessment requirements and quotes from approved assessors.
  - Start WhatsApp Business Platform verification and template drafts (lead time unknown).
  - Ask a Saudi or UAE lawyer about data transfer and hosting obligations for the Business tier.
- **Engineering (small, to protect trust):** fix reminder delivery (due-time job + web/email notification) so the product keeps its basic promise during interviews and demos.
- **Exit check:** 10 interviews logged and scored; payment route shortlisted; verification materials started.

### Weeks 3–4: Prove willingness to pay
- Finish the 25 interviews; synthesise: top three real incidents, price points, channel and language facts, objections.
- Publish the landing page; run the headline and price tests; collect waitlist with the open question.
- Invite the top 15 by commitment score to a paid pilot; collect letters of intent.
- **Gate G1 (end of week 4):** see the roadmap criteria. If it fails, do not build; re-target the segment or the problem for two more weeks.

### Weeks 5–6: Concierge MVP
- Onboard the first 5 pilot businesses on today's product plus the founder's daily supervision of extraction.
- **Engineering sprint 1:** mandatory token encryption; `actions_log` and a simple audit view; idempotency keys on send/create; per-user rate limit and budget; account deletion/export; email verification.
- **Engineering sprint 2 (starts week 6):** `commitments` collection and extraction from Gmail (reusing the current task extractor and classifier infrastructure); person-level view; manual edit/close.
- Build the **labelled evaluation set** of 200+ real consented emails in Arabic and English from pilot participants.

### Weeks 7–8: The brief and follow-ups
- Morning brief generation and delivery by email and web; time-zone aware; per-user time.
- Follow-up detection (stale "waiting on" items) and nudge drafts with one-tap approval.
- Start measuring extraction precision and recall against the evaluation set; fix prompts; add Arabic classification prompts.
- Onboard pilots 6–10.

### Weeks 9–10: Make it reliable
- Move the periodic inbox check to per-user queue jobs with Gmail incremental sync and persisted state; add retries and alerts; dead-letter handling.
- Observability: error tracking, uptime checks, cost-per-user dashboard; backups; staging environment.
- WhatsApp: connect the sandbox, send the brief via a template, receive replies; keep it behind a flag.
- Pricing page and the chosen checkout in test mode.

### Weeks 11–12: Convert and decide
- End of the four-week pilots for the first cohort: collect results (§1 ROI measures), run the survey, ask for payment.
- Switch the first paying customers to live billing.
- **Gate G2 (end of week ~12–14):** continue, pivot or stop per the roadmap criteria.
- Write the pilot case studies (with consent) and use them in outreach.

### Week 13: Plan the next 90 days
- Decide, from evidence: WhatsApp-first or email-first; whether meeting follow-ups matter more than calendar actions; which vertical (if any) clusters; hire or contract needs.

## 5. Weekly operating rhythm
- **Monday:** metrics review (activation, brief opens, ledger corrections, cost per user, support load).
- **Tuesday–Thursday:** build and customer calls (target 8+ customer conversations a week through week 12).
- **Friday:** ship, release notes to pilots, update the decision log.
- **Always:** keep a visible list of "things customers asked for" with counts; build only what appears in three or more conversations.

## 6. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Google verification or assessment delays | Without it, the product is capped at 100 users and needs weekly re-consent | Start in week 1; minimise scopes; keep pilots within the test-user limit; consider alternative providers only after checking their requirements |
| Payment collection not possible from the chosen country | No revenue | Decide in week 2; consider a merchant-of-record provider or a UAE/US entity |
| Extraction quality too low | Destroys trust | Concierge supervision, labelled set, thresholds before auto behaviour; show sources for every item |
| WhatsApp rules or costs change | The channel is part of the differentiation | Keep email as a first-class fallback; track per-message costs; avoid depending on unofficial access |
| A suite (Gmail, Copilot, Fyxer) copies the idea | Differentiation erodes | Compete on language, channel, trust and niche depth; keep shipping to a small segment; do not claim a moat you do not have |
| Founder time spread too thin | Nothing finishes | The weekly rhythm; resist modules not tied to the ledger and brief |
| Privacy incident | Fatal for trust | Encrypted tokens, least privilege, audit log, deletion, no model training on customer data, plain-language privacy page |
| Over-reading positive interviews | Builds the wrong thing | Score commitments, not compliments; the gates are numeric |

## 7. Budget sketch for the first 90 days **[Assumption — adjust]**
- Infrastructure and tools: $300–$600.
- LLM, transcription and WhatsApp usage for pilots: under $150.
- Legal and accounting advice: set aside for entity, privacy policy, DPA templates and the Saudi/UAE data question.
- Google security assessment: not payable until verification stage; get quotes in the first month.
- Founder and any contractor time is the main cost.

## 8. Definition of success at day 90
- 25+ documented interviews and a written problem statement with real incidents.
- 10 pilot businesses started, 5 or more paying.
- A working ledger, brief and approved follow-ups on real data, with measured accuracy.
- A decision at Gate G2 recorded with the evidence, not the feeling.
